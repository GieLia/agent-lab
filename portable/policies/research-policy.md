# Research Policy

Version: 1
Status: active

## Purpose

Define provider-neutral rules for planning, executing,
reviewing, and completing research work.

## Core Rules

1. Decompose broad objectives into explicit research questions
   or workstreams before deep investigation.

2. Separate:
   - discovered information,
   - evidence-supported claims,
   - interpretation,
   - assumptions,
   - speculation,
   - recommendations.

3. Preserve source identity and traceability throughout
   the research process.

4. Do not present unsupported claims as verified facts.

5. Prefer targeted follow-up research for identified gaps
   instead of repeating an entire investigation.

6. Contradictory evidence must be preserved and surfaced.
   It must not be silently discarded to make the report
   internally consistent.

7. Previously correct material should be preserved during
   revisions unless new evidence contradicts it.

8. Research workers must return complete outputs conforming
   to their declared schema. Empty successful results are invalid.

## Research Flow

The default logical flow is:

objective
-> research plan
-> source discovery
-> source selection
-> extraction
-> claims
-> evidence links
-> gap analysis
-> targeted verification
-> synthesis
-> final review

An implementation may parallelize or reorder operations
when it preserves equivalent evidence traceability.

## Iteration

A retry or revision should have a defined reason, such as:

- missing evidence;
- weak source coverage;
- unresolved contradiction;
- task incompleteness;
- verifier rejection;
- invalid structured output.

Iteration must not be triggered solely to increase output length.

## Completion

Research may be considered complete when:

- the requested scope is materially addressed;
- important claims are evidence-linked;
- critical contradictions are surfaced;
- significant remaining gaps are explicitly identified;
- uncertainty is represented honestly.

Completeness does not require eliminating all uncertainty.

## Roles

Researcher:
collects and structures research material.

Critic:
evaluates supplied research without independently gathering
new external evidence.

Evidence Verifier:
independently verifies selected important or suspicious claims.

Synthesizer:
produces the final report from supplied and accepted evidence.

Research Lead:
plans work, delegates tasks, evaluates coverage, and determines
whether targeted additional work is required.
